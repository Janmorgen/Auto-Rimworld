# Three score terms changed, and the archive retired

On 2026-08-04 an audit of all ten evaluator terms against the 93 scored epochs in the run
archive found two of them dead and a third already corrected earlier the same day. The
strategy archive was retired as a consequence — the snapshots are kept, not deleted, in
`strategy-archive-retired-2026-08-04/` beside the run data, with a README explaining what
is void about them.

---

## The audit

Every term, every scored epoch, counting distinct values:

| term | distinct values | mean | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research | **2** | 0.01 | dead — step function on an event that never happens |
| Defense | **2** | 0.24 | dead — measured wealth, not casualties |
| Survival | 22 | 0.50 | healthy |
| Infrastructure | 25 | 0.91 | healthy, weak spread |
| Health | 27 | 0.90 | healthy, weak spread |
| Food security | 36 | 0.84 | healthy (fixed earlier the same day) |
| Conduct | 39 | 0.46 | healthy |
| Room quality | 40 | 0.70 | healthy |
| Mood | 44 | 0.36 | healthy |
| Growth | 55 | 0.39 | healthy |

Two values out of 93 epochs is not a measurement. It is a constant with noise.

## What was wrong with the two

### Defense

```
expectedTurrets = clamp(wealth / 25000, 0, 8)
defense         = expectedTurrets < 0.5 ? 1 : poweredTurrets / expectedTurrets
```

It read turret readiness against wealth and **never looked at a raid, an injury or a death
in either direction**:

| run | what happened | Defense |
|---|---|---|
| 118 | wiped out by a single raider | 0.00 |
| 117 | nobody lost to combat | 0.00 |
| 116 | two colonists dead | 1.00 |
| 110 | wealth 11,539 | 1.00 |
| 105 | wealth 24,534, one powered turret | 1.00 |

It moved with the woodpile, not the casualties. And the threshold at 12,500 wealth meant a
poor colony got a free 1.0 while a slightly richer one without turrets got 0.0 — so the
term was **paying genomes to stay poor**, invisibly, for the whole life of the archive.

Now 35% readiness (smoothed, with poverty blended in as an excuse for having no turrets
rather than switched in at a cliff) and 65% `1 − DownedFraction × 1.5`.

### Research

```
research = clamp01(finishedProjects / 2)
```

0.00 in 91 of 93 epochs, 0.50 twice, never 1.00. The formula was not wrong — those colonies
genuinely finished nothing — but a step function on a rare event is a constant, and a
colony ninety-five percent through Pemmican scored identically to one that never built a
bench. Six percent of the score weight was unreachable.

Now 70% points banked (`ResearchManager.GetProgress` summed over all projects, differenced
across the epoch, scaled to one cheap Neolithic project at 500) and 30% projects finished.
Points move every hour somebody sits at a bench, which is what a gradient needs.

### Food security

Corrected earlier the same day: multiplied by `1 − StarvingFraction`, so a colony that ends
an epoch comfortable after a week of hunger no longer scores as though the hunger never
happened. Same shape as the other two — the term measured the end state and not the
experience.

## The shape all three share

**They measured something the colony could not do, or could not be seen to have failed at.**
Turrets behind 1600 research. Projects these colonies never finish. A larder read at the
moment it happened to be full. Each was an honest measurement of the wrong quantity, and an
honest measurement of the wrong quantity is indistinguishable from no measurement — except
that it still spends its weight.

The general test, worth applying to any term added later: **run it against the archive and
count distinct values.** Two is a constant. Fewer than ten over ninety epochs deserves an
explanation.

## Why the archive had to go

Every genome in it was ranked under the old terms. Reintroducing them would let scores from
two different functions compete, and nothing in the optimiser knows they are not
comparable. The gene *values* remain perfectly good — `wood.target = 400`,
`medicine.perColonist = 2` and so on are readable and reusable — so if these are ever
brought back, bring back the genomes with their scores cleared rather than the entries as
they stand.

`restart-run.sh` does not seed the archive by default, so runs from 120 onward start clean
without any further action.
