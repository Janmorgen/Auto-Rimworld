# Questing

*Part of the RimWorld core-mechanics reference. [← Back to index](index.md)*

## How Quests Work

- Quests appear in the **Quests tab** at the bottom of the screen, generated procedurally rather than scripted — no two playthroughs get the same exact quest content.
- Most are offered by factions; a smaller number come from independent/neutral sources.
- Each quest has a **difficulty rating** from 1 to 4 stars; 4-star quests are reserved for late-game/endgame content and don't scale quite like the rest.
- Quests typically run on one or two **timers**: a window to accept, and sometimes a separate window to complete once accepted.
- Rewards are usually offered as a **choice of up to three options** — commonly a mix of items, faction goodwill, and occasionally a new colonist. If you pick a goodwill/reputation reward, that's normally the only reward you get from that quest (goodwill and item rewards aren't combined, with a few exceptions).

## Common Quest Shapes

- **Site quests** — travel to ruins, a stash, or an enemy outpost via caravan; usually involves fighting through procedurally-generated threats for loot.
- **Delivery quests** — a faction requests a specific list of items by a deadline; the payment offered is always worth noticeably more than what's requested, so these are close to free profit if you have the goods on hand.
- **Defense/hosting quests** — defend a location, or host visiting guests (who may be injured, numerous, or high-maintenance) for a set duration.
- **Forced condition quests** — accept a temporary game condition (weather change, cold snap, heat wave, psychic drone) in exchange for an immediate reward.
- **Rescue/pickup quests** — a pawn (or animal, or prisoner) arrives via drop pod or shuttle and needs sheltering or safe passage.
- **Endgame quests** — tied to reaching the ship-building tech tier; these are the 4-star, one-time content that leads toward an ending.

## Practical Notes

- Accepting a quest later (right before its deadline) is often better than accepting immediately, since the completion timer usually only starts counting once you accept — this buys prep time.
- Declining a quest is normally free; there's rarely a penalty for letting the offer expire unaccepted.
- Failure conditions vary by quest type — letting the acceptance timer run out, failing to deliver goods in time, or losing a required pawn/building are the most common ways to fail an accepted quest.
- Quests are one of the few sources of quest-exclusive rewards that can't otherwise be crafted or bought, alongside new colonists and large lump sums of goodwill/silver.
