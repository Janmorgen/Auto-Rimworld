# Food

*Part of the RimWorld core-mechanics reference. [← Back to index](index.md)*

Cooking, meal quality, and storage. For the underlying hunger need and raw eating, see [Nutrition](nutrition.md).

## Meal Tiers (Cooking work, best to most basic)

| Meal | Mood effect | Ingredients | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lavish meal | Biggest bonus | Most, including fine ingredients | Top-tier, resource-intensive |
| Fine meal | Strong bonus | Moderate | The everyday "good" meal |
| Simple meal | Small bonus | Standard | The default meal type, low food-poisoning risk when made well |
| Pemmican | Mood penalty if eaten often | Meat + plant matter | Barely spoils — the caravan/stockpile staple. Needs Pemmican research |
| Packaged survival meal | Neutral | Pre-made | Never spoils, no cook needed. Needs Nutrient paste research |
| Nutrient paste | Real mood penalty every time | Any raw ingredients, including inedible ones | Instant, automatic, never runs dry if supplied |

## Cooking Mechanics

- **Cooking skill** governs meal quality odds, food-poisoning chance, and butchery efficiency (how much meat/leather you get per corpse).
- A Cooking skill of roughly 9 minimizes food-poisoning chance outright; higher levels beyond that mostly just add speed.
- Meals are cooked at a **campfire** (no fuel/power needed, always available) or an **electric stove** (needs power, faster/better) — see [Crafting](crafting.md) for the bench list.
- A **Nutrient Paste Dispenser** bypasses cooking entirely, converting stored raw ingredients (including otherwise-inedible ones) directly into an instant meal at zero labor cost, at the price of a mood penalty per serving.

## Food Poisoning

- Can come from undercooked/raw food, a low-skill cook, or a dirty kitchen.
- A clean kitchen (see [Room Attributes](room-attributes.md)) further reduces the chance on top of cook skill.
- A food-poisoned pawn suffers vomiting and reduced capacities for a while but usually recovers without treatment.

## Storage & Spoilage

- Most raw food and cooked meals spoil over time unless refrigerated; a **freezer** (a room held below 0°C/32°F) is the standard long-term solution — see [Room Types](room-types.md).
- Pemmican and packaged survival meals are the two shelf-stable exceptions, making them the standard choice for caravans and emergency reserves.
- Deep-frozen or otherwise unspoiled food still needs periodic restocking for a growing colony — planning surplus growing/hunting capacity ahead of population growth avoids a sudden shortfall.
