# Biomes

*Part of the RimWorld core-mechanics reference. [← Back to index](index.md)*

Biome is set when you pick a landing site and can't be changed afterward — it defines your baseline temperature, soil fertility, wildlife, and disease risk for the whole game.

## Warm/Temperate Biomes

- **Temperate forest** — widely considered the most forgiving start: fertile soil, moderate temperatures, plenty of wood and wildlife, relatively low disease.
- **Temperate swamp** — a wetter version of the above; even more fertile ground but marshy terrain that slows movement and construction, plus higher disease frequency.
- **Tropical rainforest** — year-round growing season and abundant wildlife/fertility, offset by frequent disease (malaria, sleeping sickness, and plague all show up here).
- **Tropical swamp** — combines tropical rainforest's fertility with swamp terrain penalties and even more disease.
- **Arid shrubland** — a dry transition zone; sparser vegetation and lower fertility, but comfortable temperatures and fewer predators/diseases than the forest biomes.

## Cold Biomes

- **Boreal forest** — cooler than temperate forest with a shorter growing season, but still fairly forgiving; dense pine/birch and decent wildlife.
- **Tundra** — short growing season, tolerable cold, sparser resources than boreal forest.
- **Ice sheet** — essentially no growing season and minimal wildlife; survival leans heavily on hydroponics, hunting what little exists, and imported food.
- **Sea ice** — a similarly harsh, coastal-flavored variant.

## Hot/Dry Biomes

- **Desert** — low fertility, hot, sparse wood; outdoor farming is difficult without workarounds like hydroponics.
- **Extreme desert** — desert taken further; only the most fertility-tolerant crops (like potatoes) do even marginally well outdoors, and heat management is a constant fight.

## What Biome Actually Controls

- **Growing season length** — from year-round down to a short window, directly limiting outdoor farming; see [Plants](plants.md).
- **Soil fertility** — how much crop yield/growth speed you get per tile before hydroponics.
- **Wildlife density and species** — which animals you can hunt or tame; see [Animals](animals.md).
- **Disease frequency** — swamps and tropical biomes roll for disease far more often than tundra or desert; see [Medicine](medicine.md).
- **Temperature extremes** — how much heating/cooling infrastructure you'll need to keep colonists and crops safe; see [Base Building](base-building.md) and [Power](power.md).
