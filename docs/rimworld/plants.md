# Plant Types

*Part of the RimWorld core-mechanics reference. [← Back to index](index.md)*

Growth speed scales with soil fertility, light, and temperature (roughly 10–42°C / 50–108°F is the normal growth range for most crops). Exact yield/day numbers shift slightly between game versions, so figures below are relative comparisons rather than fixed values — treat them as planning guidance, not exact math.

## Wild (Foraged) Plants & Trees

Not player-sown, but common resource sources found across biomes:

- **Berry bush** — a common wild food source in temperate/boreal biomes
- **Agave, saguaro cactus, pincushion cactus** — desert/arid-biome food and forage
- **Wild healroot** — free medicine ingredient in some biomes
- **Ambrosia bush** — rare, gives a strong short-term mood/recreation boost when eaten
- **Grass, tall grass, bush, low shrubs, moss** — general terrain flora, mostly aesthetic/forage
- **Trees** (harvested for wood): oak, pine, birch, maple, palm, cypress, willow, teak, poplar, bamboo — species vary by biome

## Mechanics Worth Knowing

- **Soil fertility** ranges from around 10% (sand) up through 100% (normal soil) to 140% (rich soil) to 280% (hydroponics basin). A plant's own fertility sensitivity decides how much this matters — potatoes barely care, rice and corn care a lot.
- **Hydroponics basins** need power and light (a sun lamp if roofed) but grow most eligible crops dramatically faster than soil. Haygrass and Devilstrand cannot be grown hydroponically.
- **Temperature**: growth is normal between roughly 10–42°C; below 10°C growth slows sharply, and above 42°C it also drops off. Frost can kill crops outright.
- **Blight** can infect and kill outdoor crops in a spreading patch — cut infected plants promptly to contain it.
- All plants stop growing overnight (roughly an 11-hour window) regardless of other conditions.
- Growing skill matters: it sets sow/harvest speed, and a handful of plants (Devilstrand, for example) have a minimum Plants skill requirement before a colonist is even allowed to sow them.
